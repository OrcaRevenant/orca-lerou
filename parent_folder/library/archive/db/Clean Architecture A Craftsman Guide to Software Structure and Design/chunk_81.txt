You might object to these solutions on the basis that every class would contain
just one function. This is hardly the case. The number of functions required to
calculate pay, generate a report, or save the data is likely to be large in each case.
Each of those classes would have many private methods in them.
Each of the classes that contain such a family of methods is a scope. Outside of
that scope, no one knows that the private members of the family exist.
CONCLUSION
The Single Responsibility Principle is about functions and classes—but it
reappears in a different form at two more levels. At the level of components, it
becomes the Common Closure Principle. At the architectural level, it becomes
the Axis of Change responsible for the creation of Architectural Boundaries.
We’ll be studying all of these ideas in the chapters to come.
